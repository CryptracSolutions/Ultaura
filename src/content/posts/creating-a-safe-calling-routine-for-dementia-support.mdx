For dementia support, consistency often matters more than complexity. A safe calling routine should feel familiar, calm, and predictable for both the older adult and the family.

When call structure changes every day, confusion rises. When the same rhythm repeats, stress usually drops.

## Start with Safety Foundations

### Pick One Primary Call Window

Choose a time that matches the person's best hours of focus. For many families, a late-morning or early-evening window is easier than late night.

Keep the window consistent for at least two weeks before making changes.

### Prepare the Environment

Before calls, reduce avoidable confusion:

- Lower TV or background noise
- Keep hearing aids and glasses nearby
- Use the same room or chair when possible
- Keep a simple reminder card by the phone

### Define Your Backup Path

Set a clear fallback sequence in advance:

1. First retry after a short gap
2. Second retry in a backup window
3. Contact trusted person A
4. Contact trusted person B
5. Escalate to urgent care steps if needed

In Ultaura, this is easier to maintain when schedules, quiet hours, and trusted contacts are configured in one place.

## Use a Repeatable Call Structure

Use the same call flow each time. This lowers cognitive load.

### Four-Part Call Flow

1. Orientation cue (30 to 60 seconds)
   - "Hi Dad, it's Anna. Today is Tuesday morning. I'm calling for our check-in."
2. Safety and comfort check (2 to 3 minutes)
   - "Did you eat? Do you need water? Any pain right now?"
3. One small plan (2 to 4 minutes)
   - "After lunch, let's take a short walk in the hallway."
4. Warm close with next touchpoint (30 to 60 seconds)
   - "I will call again at 7:00 PM. You're not alone."

### Helpful Prompt Style

Prefer short, concrete prompts:

- "Would you like tea or water?" instead of open-ended choices
- "Let's do one step now" instead of "What should we do today?"
- "I am calling at our usual time" instead of "Do you remember why I called?"

## Language Choices That Reduce Confusion

### Do

- Speak slowly and pause
- Use names and time anchors ("this morning", "after dinner")
- Ask one question at a time
- Validate feelings before correcting facts

### Avoid

- Rapid topic switching
- Quizzing memory ("You remember this, right?")
- Arguing over details
- Long multi-step instructions

## Weekly Routine Checklist

Run this checklist once each week:

- Confirm call times still match the person's strongest hours
- Review missed calls and update the retry plan
- Refresh reminder cards near the phone
- Check if hearing, vision, or device issues affected calls
- Reconfirm trusted contact order and phone numbers
- Note any new behavior changes to discuss with professionals

## If Confusion Is Increasing

Adjust quickly, but make one change at a time:

- Shorten calls
- Move calls earlier in the day
- Increase repetition and reduce choices
- Add a second short check-in instead of one long call

If new confusion appears suddenly, or safety concerns rise, contact a licensed clinician promptly.

> Not medical advice: This guidance is educational and should be used alongside recommendations from licensed healthcare professionals.

## The Goal: Calm, Not Perfect

A good routine does not require perfect conversations. It requires predictable connection, clear safety checks, and a reliable backup path when a call is missed.
