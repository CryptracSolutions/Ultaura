The first two weeks matter more than any feature list.

If your parent feels respected and comfortable early, check-in calls become part of daily life instead of "one more thing" to manage. This plan helps you build that trust in small, practical steps.

## Before Day 1: Set expectations together

Do this in one short conversation, ideally in person.

Explain that Ultaura is there to support independence, routine, and peace of mind. Keep the tone collaborative.

### Use this opening script

"I want to make our check-ins easier on both of us. We can set this up your way, and change anything that feels off."

### Decide three basics together

1. Preferred call window (not exact minute yet).
2. Preferred call tone (brief, friendly, practical, or chatty).
3. What should happen if a call is missed.

## Days 1-3: Make the first calls easy wins

Your only goal is comfort.

Keep calls short. Avoid testing too many settings. End each call with appreciation so the routine feels positive.

### Daily checklist (Days 1-3)

- Confirm they can hear clearly.
- Keep call length to `3-5` minutes.
- Ask one practical and one personal question.
- Confirm tomorrow's call window.
- Note any friction (timing, volume, pace).

### Questions that build momentum

- "Was this call time convenient today?"
- "Did this feel too short, too long, or about right?"
- "Would you rather have reminders in a different style?"

## Days 4-7: Add gentle structure

Once the first few calls feel normal, add one more layer: consistency.

Now is the right time to lock in a stable window and define the missed-call plan clearly.

### Family setup tasks for Days 4-7

1. Choose one primary call window for weekdays.
2. Choose one backup window for missed calls.
3. Add at least one trusted contact as backup.
4. Agree on what counts as "urgent" versus "can wait until tomorrow."

### Keep autonomy front and center

Ask for preferences before making changes.

Instead of "We changed your settings," try "We noticed evenings felt rushed. Want to move calls 30 minutes later?"

## Days 8-10: Personalize for real life

By now, you have enough signal to personalize without guessing.

Small adjustments can make calls feel less generic and more supportive.

### What to personalize

- Preferred greeting name.
- Topics they enjoy discussing.
- Topics to avoid.
- Best pace for reminders.
- Quiet hours and no-call periods.

### Example of respectful personalization

"You mentioned mornings are hectic on Tuesdays. Should we skip that slot and use the afternoon instead?"

## Days 11-14: Review outcomes and stabilize

End the first 14 days with a calm review, not a big overhaul.

Look for what is already working, then make one or two targeted improvements.

### 14-day review checklist

- Completion rate: Were most scheduled calls completed?
- Comfort: Did your parent report the calls felt helpful?
- Timing: Is the call window still practical?
- Escalation: Did everyone understand the missed-call plan?
- Caregiver load: Did this reduce manual coordination stress?

## Common early issues and fixes

### "The calls feel repetitive"

Rotate prompts by day of week. Keep one anchor question, change the rest.

### "Timing keeps drifting"

Narrow the window and protect it like any standing appointment.

### "My parent feels monitored"

Re-state the shared goal: connection and confidence. Let your parent choose one setting each week.

### "Family members disagree on urgency"

Write a simple escalation rubric with clear triggers and time thresholds.

## Sample 14-day message cadence for caregivers

Use this cadence in family chat so everyone stays aligned:

- Day 1: "Setup complete, keeping calls short this week."
- Day 4: "Primary and backup windows confirmed."
- Day 8: "Personalization updates made based on feedback."
- Day 14: "Review done, routine is stable, next check-in in 30 days."

## Final takeaway

A successful Ultaura start is less about technology and more about trust.

Protect dignity, keep changes small, and review progress after two weeks. When families pace onboarding this way, consistency comes naturally.
